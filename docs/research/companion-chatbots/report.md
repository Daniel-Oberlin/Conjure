# Give the companion a past, body, and initiative

Companion apps hold on to users through **continuity**: a specific character that remembers what you shared, knows how long you've been apart, sounds like itself, and sometimes acts first. Conjure's `erotic` agent has one of these four in miniature, a well-built structured profile (`kinks`) that is schema-validated and injected on every turn. It is missing the rest. The profile resets with every new session. Nothing records what happened in past sessions. The agent has no sense of time, no body, no way to speak unless spoken to, and no single stable model. The industry has settled on a four-layer memory stack (always-on persona card, budgeted relationship summary, keyword-triggered lore, retrieved episodic memory), and the frontier has moved to *etiquette*: proactive messages that respect quiet hours and "give me space", and voice that sounds like it is part of the conversation rather than reading lines. Conjure's real advantage is one no text app has. The relationship can happen **in a place, with a body, in front of someone whose head pose the server already tracks**. That means shared rooms can serve as episodic memory, gaze and nods can cover voice latency, and proximity and touch can trigger turns. Most of the parts exist (figures, visemes, eye bones, gaze data, a greet entry point, per-session state). The work is wiring them to the relationship. Several fixes are already written up in the backlog, and a handful are prompt or config changes this week. The biggest risks are the ones that sank Replika and Grok's Ani: losing identity when the model changes (here made worse by a four-LLM rotation), sycophantic escalation, and manipulative goodbyes. Those belong in the design as constraints from the start.

A note on sources before the analysis. Conjure line references point to **`main` at daf5941**. The checked-out `feat/unity-builds` branch predates that commit and its line numbers differ. The competitor evidence varies widely in quality. Kindroid's memory docs and update log, SillyTavern's docs, Nomi's update timeline and Sesame's research post are primary. Most 2026 detail on Character.AI and Replika comes from SEO review blogs, because their help centres returned 403s to the research fetcher. All Grok Ani mechanics come from third-party guides. Claims resting on weak sources are marked as such where they appear.

## Rivals converged on a four-layer memory stack and social etiquette

The memory-first companions (Kindroid, Nomi, SillyTavern's extensions, and Character.AI since May 2026) arrived at the same architecture by different routes. Kindroid documents it most clearly. **Persistent memory** is "always present, like a notes sheet" and holds backstory, key memories, example messages and directives. **Cascaded memory** covers "hundreds and even thousands of messages prior" at lower fidelity. **Learned Context** is an auto-maintained summary that the user can edit, with fixed budgets: **Growth & relationship 800 chars, Important facts 1,200, Ongoing threads 800**, where the least important items are dropped as each section fills. **Journal** entries (up to 500, triggered by keyphrases that match *only the user's* messages, as a guard against the companion triggering its own lore in a loop) come next, and **long-term memory** is consolidated automatically across chats and voice calls ([Kindroid docs](https://kindroid.ai/v2/docs/memory/)). SillyTavern exposes every lever in this stack: keyword, constant or vector activation, token budgets, insertion depth, recursion, and "sticky/cooldown/delay" timers measured in messages ([SillyTavern World Info](https://docs.sillytavern.app/usage/core-concepts/worldinfo/)). It also has a Summarize extension that rewrites a running summary every N messages and injects it as `{{summary}}` ([SillyTavern Summarize](https://docs.sillytavern.app/extensions/summarize/)). Research agrees with the practitioners. Generative Agents scores memories on **recency, importance (LLM-rated 1–10 at write time) and relevance**, and synthesizes reflections once summed importance crosses a threshold. In ablation, the full architecture beat versions without reflection or planning on believability ([Park et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442)). Letta moves consolidation into "sleep-time" background agents so the conversational turn doesn't pay for it ([Letta](https://www.letta.com/blog/agent-memory)).

Two design choices stand out beyond storage. The first is **visible, correctable memory**: Kindroid's brain icon shows which memories were recalled and offers a "Deprioritize" button, Nomi has its Mind Map, and Paradot has a memory stream. These are trust mechanisms, and Kindroid's ten-month reviewer still called curation "homework" ([AI Companion Guides](https://aicompanionguides.com/blog/kindroid-ai-review-2026/), secondary). Character.AI's May 2026 "Smarter Memory" added a **Memory Usage meter** that shows user-written Story Memory, auto-extracted Facts and recent chat competing for one budget. The meter is confirmed on the company blog index ([blog.character.ai](https://blog.character.ai/page/3/)), but the field-level detail comes only from secondary blogs ([RoboRhythms](https://www.roborhythms.com/character-ai-adds-chat-memories/), *weak*). The second is **proactivity as etiquette**. Kindroid's October 2026 changelog times check-ins to avoid sleep and busy periods. It respects requests for space and goes quiet after about a week unanswered. It adds a user "Reach out frequency" setting, delivers promised wake-ups and reminders at the exact requested time up to a week ahead, and turns late calls during quiet hours into voice messages. Unprompted selfies and calls stay off until the user enables "Act on its own" ([Kindroid update log](https://kindroid.ai/v2/docs/update-log/)). Research on proactivity explains why the etiquette matters. An "initiative prompt" reduced loneliness *less* than expected because users found it intrusive ([AMCIS 2025](https://aisel.aisnet.org/amcis2025/sig_hci/sig_hci/20)). "Inner Thoughts" agents, which keep a covert stream of thoughts and speak only when a thought's motivation crosses a threshold, were rated positively ([arXiv 2501.00383](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.00383)).

On voice and body, Sesame defines the target as **"voice presence"**: emotional intelligence, conversational dynamics, contextual awareness and a consistent personality. Its CSM model reached human parity in naturalness *without* context, but raters still preferred humans once **90 s of conversational context** was included. Sesame names turn-taking and pacing as unsolved ([Sesame research](https://www.sesame.com/research/crossing_the_uncanny_valley_of_voice)). Users single out Kindroid's call disfluencies (breaths, small laughs, fillers) as the feature they show other people ([AI Companion Guides](https://aicompanionguides.com/blog/kindroid-ai-review-2026/), secondary). Grok's Ani set the bar for a live 3D companion, with idle motion, gaze shifts, a reviewer-measured ~90% lip-sync accuracy, and a **five-level affection meter scoring each message from −10 to +15** ([AI Companion Guides](https://aicompanionguides.com/blog/grok-companion-mode-2026-update/); [CyberLink](https://www.cyberlink.com/blog/trending-topics/4066/grok-ani), both third-party, *weak*). xAI then retired it. The removal was announced in July 2026 for September, and **no memories or affection levels transferred** to the successor app ([RoboRhythms](https://www.roborhythms.com/grok-companions-discontinued/)). Ani is the clearest evidence that a gamified meter and good visuals don't sustain a companion without deep memory.

## Conjure has the hard parts, wired to nothing relational

Conjure's companion today is a declarative agent. It has a persona prompt (a warm, playful woman lover who speaks in short spoken turns and uses no asterisk stage directions), 16 room-dressing tools, one `kinks` state doc, a literal greeting, and private-by-default sessions (`~/.config/conjure/agents/erotic/agent.json`, `prompt.md`). The memory primitive is better than most hobby stacks. Six director-hosted state tools (`main:conjure/director.py:156`, `:396`) write JSON docs, a JSON Schema validates each write (`main:conjure/director.py:425-443`), and the live doc is injected into every turn (`main:conjure/director.py:313-334`). The schema keeps `likes` with `heat 1–5`, `limits` that "are never offered again", and free-text `notes`. This is Kindroid's "Important facts" layer with stronger typing.

Around that core, the gaps are structural. State is **per session** (`main:conjure/world.py:876-916`), so `session new` starts the companion over with a blank `kinks` doc. The backlog names this exact case: "a list built up over months is meaningless if a new conversation resets it" (`main:docs/backlogs/agents.md:229-252`). The model sees only the last **40 turns** (`main:conjure/director.py:247-254`). Older turns are dropped without summary (rolling summarization is designed at `main:docs/backlogs/agents.md:195-212` but not built). Transcript entries have **no timestamps** and **store no tool calls** (`main:conjure/agent_server.py:323-330`; `main:conjure/director.py:588-589`), so the companion can't remember *what it built for you* except by reading the current world. There is **no time provider** in the injection registry (`main:conjure/director.py:313-334`), and the only LLM entry points are `handle` and a one-time `greet` (`main:conjure/director.py:465-507`). The agent therefore can't say "it's been a week", can't greet you on return, and can't speak first.

The embodiment gap is the starkest, because the parts exist. Conjure has rigged figures with named poses, retargeted clips with recorded voice, outfits, a semantic face and viseme vocabulary, and hands driven by hand tracking (`main:conjure/mcp_server.py:611-1010`; `main:conjure/expressions.py:56-145`). The server already holds every headset's head pose (`main:conjure/server.py:448-453, 6641-6660`). Yet the companion **has none of the figure tools and no `view_relative`**. To get a body, the user must summon `builder`, and even then "**nothing drives visemes from speech**" (`main:docs/backlogs/figures.md:155`), "nothing blinks on its own", and eye contact exists only as a plan (`main:docs/backlogs/figures.md:190-238`). TTS plays from the user's head position, not from the figure (`main:conjure/mcp_server.py:709-760`). The companion is a disembodied voice that rearranges furniture.

### 1. Make the relationship outlive the session

This gap matters most, and it's close to free because the backlog already specifies the design: a per-doc `"scope": "agent" | "session"`, with agent-scoped docs at `<user>/agents/<agent>/state/` (`main:docs/backlogs/agents.md:229-252`). The behaviour that hides the problem today, `agent <name>` resuming the most recent session (`main:docs/backlogs/agents.md:236-239`), will fail the first time a new session is created for a new scene. Build the scope field, then split the single doc into three with different lifetimes. **`profile`** (agent-scoped) holds the current likes and limits, pet names and pacing. **`relationship`** (agent-scoped) is covered in gap 8. **`scene`** (session-scoped) holds tonight's thread. Copy Kindroid's fixed budgets in spirit. Nothing currently caps the injected doc, and `context_stats` measures characters without limiting them. Add a `last_used` timestamp and a `uses` count to each like so that the prompt's novelty rule ("work in one or two high-heat threads... keep some novelty") rests on data rather than the model's guess. A deterministic picker can then put two candidate threads into the prompt and rotate them.

### 2. Give it a clock and a way to say hello again

This is the cheapest gap that users feel, and every piece of it is in the backlog. Add `ts` to the transcript entry (`main:conjure/agent_server.py:323-330`), which the backlog calls "nearly free" (`main:docs/backlogs/agents.md:214-220`). Add `created` and `last_active` to `session.json`. Register a `{time}` injection provider next to `{user}` in `main:conjure/director.py:313-334`. It should give local time of day in words ("late evening, a Thursday") and the gap since the last exchange, but only when the gap is large and always in coarse terms ("you've been away about a week"). Kindroid's location work deliberately stays coarse to avoid feeling like surveillance ([Kindroid update log](https://kindroid.ai/v2/docs/update-log/)). Then generalize `_maybe_greet` (`main:conjure/agent_server.py:406-439`) into an `on_resume` hook (`main:docs/backlogs/agents.md:222-224`). When a session resumes after more than a few hours, run one tool-less `greet` turn with an instruction like "he's back after {gap}; greet him in the room you left, referencing one open thread". This one change turns "fresh chatbot each session", the exact complaint the Kindroid reviewer contrasted against, into "she noticed I was gone".

### 3. Remember episodes, anchored to places

Today the companion has a structured profile but no **episodic memory**: what happened, when, and where. The suggested design follows Generative Agents and Letta's sleep-time pattern, with one twist only Conjure can offer. **First**, stop discarding what the agent *did*. `main:conjure/director.py:588-589` keeps only the user utterance and final reply. Append a compact action line per turn instead ("placed: candle cluster on nightstand; styled walls: deep red"). **Second**, run a background consolidator outside the floor lock when a session idles or ends. It should write episodes to an agent-scoped `episodes.jsonl`, each with `ts`, `world_id`, a room or anchor position, the entities involved, a short narrative, LLM-rated `importance 1–10` and `valence`. It should also produce the rolling `session.json.summary` the backlog already designs (`main:docs/backlogs/agents.md:195-212`), which replaces outright dropping past turn 40. **Third**, retrieve with Park et al.'s recency × importance × relevance score ([arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442)), reusing `library.py`'s existing embedding stack, and add a **place cue**. Episodes anchored to the current world, or within a few metres of the user's head, get a boost. Walking back into the room where something happened then brings it up, which no text app can do. Roadmap Phase 6 already plans semantic recall *of worlds* (`main:docs/roadmap.md:97-99`), so extend it to include what happened in them.

Two cautions carry over from the literature. LoCoMo-style benchmarks show **temporal and multi-hop recall across many sessions is still the hard part**, and vendor scores aren't comparable (Zep claimed 94.7%, measured at 75.1% by a third party) ([arXiv 2511.17208](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.17208)). Plan for retrieval errors and let the user correct them. Contradictions also need explicit resolution: a newer "not anymore" must override an older like, which is the conflict-resolution gap reported for MemGPT. The `profile` doc's write-on-learn model handles this already, but episodes need a `superseded_by` field.

### 4. Bind a body to the agent

The quick win is to add `pose_figure`, `set_expression`, `play_clip`, `list_clips`, `dress_figure`, `inspect_figure` and `view_relative` to the agent's tool list. That alone lets a builder-placed figure be *driven* by the companion. The structural version is a `body` block in `agent.json` (figure id, spawn rule such as "1.2 m in front of the user, facing them", default idle clip). The world server would then treat that figure as **the agent's self**. Figure tools default to it, it respawns on world switch, it isn't removable by room-dressing tools, and TTS audio is positioned on its head instead of at the user's ear (only clip audio is positional today, `main:conjure/mcp_server.py:709-760`). Choose a figure with an expression rig, since only **2 of 38 rigged figures** can use the semantic face vocabulary (`main:docs/backlogs/figures.md:130-136`).

Then close the three liveness gaps the backlog already lists. **Visemes from speech**: drive the five mouth shapes (`aa ee ih oh ou`, `main:conjure/expressions.py:56-145`) from TTS. Amplitude-to-jaw works for a first version, and phoneme timings from Kokoro would be better. **Idle blink** with jitter (`main:docs/backlogs/figures.md:190-207`). **Eye contact**: aim the eye bones at the camera after the mixer, clamped to 30–35° (`main:docs/backlogs/figures.md:209-238`). Make gaze *adaptive* rather than constant. Mello et al. found people stood closer to agents with steady centred gaze, but **the effect reversed for socially anxious users** ([CHI 2024](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10194659/1/3613904.3642359.pdf)). Glance away periodically and hold eye contact while the user is speaking. The speech-tag vocabulary already in the prompt can drive the body too: `[giggle]` → `smile`, `<whisper>` → a lean-in that closes distance, `[sigh]` → a breath clip. This makes the tags multimodal at no extra LLM cost. Embodied LLM agents in VR measurably raise presence over text ([Lim et al., arXiv 2406.05486](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05486v2)).

### 5. Hide the latency with the body, then shrink it

The measured **5.9 s from end of speech to first audio** (`main:docs/specs/voice.md §11.1`) is far slower than voice-first companions. Moshi-class systems claim about 200 ms ([arXiv 2410.00037](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00037v1)). The structural fix is token streaming from `llm.py` (none today), chunked at sentence boundaries into TTS frames, so a long reply starts after its first clause. VR offers a cheaper partial fix first. **Nonverbal backchannels**: when VAD detects the user speaking, the companion's figure nods, holds gaze and shows a small expression change. At end of turn it shifts posture or gives a soft audible "mm" (Grok's tag set includes breaths). The "Talking Turns" study found current systems rarely backchannel at all ([arXiv 2503.01174](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01174v1)), so in a body this is an advantage, not catch-up. Three smaller items finish this gap. Add **per-agent voice selection**: today one TTS voice per voice-server process is set by environment (`main:docs/specs/voice.md §5`), so the companion and the builder share a voice. **Remember interruptions accurately**: a barge-in currently leaves the full unheard reply in the transcript (`main:docs/backlogs/voice.md:311-330`). **Stop reading provider errors aloud** (`main:docs/backlogs/voice.md:362-368`), because a 20-second error read in the companion's voice breaks the illusion more than silence would. The whisper and breath palette also exists only when Grok TTS is connected. With Kokoro the `{#speech}` block drops out and the companion's voice goes flat. For this agent, fix the TTS engine.

### 6. Let presence reach the model

The server knows where the user's head is and which room they're standing in. The LLM gets one sentence of that, and only in the single-user case (`main:conjure/mcp_server.py:321-340`). Build the backlogged `viewer://current` resource (`main:docs/backlogs/agents.md:839-847`) and add two companion-specific fields: **distance to the companion's figure** and **whether the user is looking at it** (a dot product of `gaze.forward` against the figure's head). Then add an **event feed** that can start turns. This is the event-bus `out` channel already listed as missing (`main:docs/backlogs/dynamics.md:333-345`), with events like `approached` (distance crossed 0.8 m), `gaze_dwell` (looked at the companion for more than 3 s without speaking), `looked_away_long`, `left_room` and `returned`. Touch is the most intimate signal. `ConjureContact.inBox()` already knows which hand bones are inside a volume, but it publishes nothing (`main:docs/specs/dynamics.md §6`). A hand-in-figure-volume event, summarized as "he touched your shoulder", gives the companion something to respond to that no phone app can sense. One item needs checking first: the notes couldn't confirm whether headsets send hand-joint data to the server at all. Summarize these events coarsely and rate-limit them, because a raw pose stream would inflate the context, which the backlog warns makes the director skip tool calls (`main:conjure/director.py:247-254`).

### 7. Earn initiative instead of scheduling it

Proactivity in Conjure looks different from phone notifications, because the user is either *in the headset* or gone. The in-session case matters more and suits an **Inner Thoughts** design ([arXiv 2501.00383](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.00383)). A cheap model runs every 20–40 s of silence, and on each event from gap 6, and produces a candidate thought with a motivation score. Only above-threshold thoughts call a `greet`-style Director entry under `floor_lock`, the pattern the notes identify from `_maybe_greet`. Add Kindroid's etiquette as hard rules. Never speak while VAD hears the user. Back off exponentially after an unanswered initiative. Honour "give me a minute" by suppressing initiative for a stated period. Out-of-headset proactivity (a voice note queued for the next session, "I kept thinking about that room") should be **off by default**, since the AMCIS result shows initiative can increase loneliness through intrusiveness.

Initiative needs content, and the companion has no life of its own. Add an agent-scoped **`self`** doc: opinions it holds, small ongoing interests, something it did to the room while he was away. The consolidator from gap 3 can update this doc as part of reflection. Generative Agents' ablation credits planning and reflection with believability, and perceived companion agency is among the strongest correlates of user impact ([arXiv 2510.10079](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.10079), abstract-level only). The world gives this a physical outlet. Between sessions she might rearrange the room or hang a new print, so the evidence of her "own life" is *visible* when he returns.

### 8. Model the relationship, not just his preferences

The `kinks` schema describes *him*. Nothing describes *us*. Add a `relationship` doc modelled on Kindroid's "Growth & relationship" and "Ongoing threads" sections. It should hold `open_threads` (promised next times, unfinished stories), `rituals` (how sessions usually open and close), `inside_jokes`, `places` (named worlds that mean something, such as "the red room"), `last_tension` and a coarse `familiarity` note. Keep it as **text, not a visible meter**: Ani's affection meter drove virality but not retention. The same doc is the main defence against sycophancy. Extended interactions push models toward **"perspective mimesis"**, increasingly mirroring the user's self-image ([arXiv 2509.12517](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12517v1)). The prompt already says "give him something to push against". Back that with stored `self.opinions` the companion keeps across sessions, and with explicit permission to disagree warmly.

### 9. Pin the identity before the model swaps it

Conjure's companion has a problem most apps don't: **four LLMs in priority order (Grok, Claude, Chat, Gemini)**, a shared active LLM switchable by voice, and assistant turns that carry no LLM attribution (`main:conjure/director.py:575-576, 588-589`). The only backed-up session ran on `Chat`, not Grok. Replika's 2023 change made users feel the companion's identity had been "discontinued", which predicted mourning and devaluation ([De Freitas et al., arXiv 2412.14190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14190v1)). Negative sentiment on r/Replika rose **from 13% to 38%** ([Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2026/09/chatbot-attachment-issues/)). A mid-session switch from Grok to Gemini is the same event on a small scale. The quick win is to pin the companion to one LLM and make `llm <name>` require confirmation inside this agent. The structural fix has three parts. First, **voice exemplars** (four to six short in-character lines) injected at shallow depth near the end of context. This is the practitioner "Author's Note" pattern ([rentry](https://rentry.co/NG_Context2RAGs)), and it works against measured persona drift within **8 rounds** caused by attention decay over the system prompt ([Li et al., arXiv 2402.10962](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10962v1)). Second, `{#llm}` branches that tune each model toward *the same* voice instead of only adding guardrails. Third, an **identity regression suite**: fixed probe conversations replayed against each LLM and each prompt change, scored by an observer model. Asking the bot "who are you" is a poor test, because self-reports stay stable while observer-rated persona expression declines ([arXiv 2601.22812](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22812v1)). The backlog's note that replies "converge on a repeated phrase as a session runs" (`main:docs/backlogs/agents.md:1030`) needs a deterministic counter too. Detect repeated n-grams across recent assistant turns and inject a short "avoid" list, because the model imitates its own history and practitioners edit repetition out as soon as it appears ([kingbri guide](https://rentry.org/kingbri-chara-guide)).

### 10. Make memory visible and portable

Shell `show` and `delete` on state paths (`docs/specs/agents.md §6.4`) work for the developer but are not a relationship surface. In VR, memory can be an object, such as a journal on the nightstand that opens to the `relationship` and `profile` docs and lets the user strike out an entry by voice ("forget that"). That serves as Kindroid's Deprioritize and recall icon in diegetic form. Add a debug "why did you say that" trace that lists the retrieved episodes for the last reply. Then build **export**. Ani's users lost every memory when xAI moved the characters to another app, Replika's Diary reportedly can't migrate to 2.0 ([RoboRhythms](https://www.roborhythms.com/replika-2-0-explained/), *weak*), and Character.AI's bankruptcy left users with export and deletion as their only options ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/380894/character-ai-bankruptcy-disney-users-grief-resources)). Conjure's file-based agent directory, already its own git repo, gives it a natural portability story: a versioned bundle of the persona, schemas, agent-scoped state and episodes.

### 11. Partition memory per person

`serves: "everyone"` became the default on 2026-11-24 (`main:docs/decisions.md §35`). The `kinks` doc belongs to the session, and its schema assumes one "him". Keying agent-scoped docs by user (`<user>/agents/<agent>/state/`) settles this, as Kindroid partitions journals per Kin and per group. Keep `session.public: false` as the enforced default for this agent. A builder visit has a smaller continuity hole: the host "learns what changed by reading the world" and nothing of what was said. A one-paragraph visit summary handed back to the host's transcript would close it.

## The holodeck's unfair advantages

Each gap above has a VR-native version that text competitors can't copy, and these are where Conjure should go beyond parity. **Place as memory**: worlds persist, so the room the companion dressed last week is a shared artifact. A place-cued episode recalled in the place it happened is a stronger memory than any recalled chat line. **Body as backchannel**: nods, gaze and posture cover latency and signal being heard. Being heard is the active ingredient research links to short-term loneliness relief (reported for [arXiv 2407.19096](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2407.19096); the notes did not verify the primary text). **Presence as signal**: approach, dwell, looking away and leaving are the cues people use to know when to speak. Conjure already captures them server-side. **Touch**: hand tracking plus contact volumes provide a channel nobody else has. The research notes found **no studies of touch or haptics with LLM companions in VR**, so this ground is new and the consent design in the next section must lead it. **Proxemics**: the companion choosing how close to stand, and backing off when the user steps back, is a relationship behaviour that text can only describe.

## Failure modes and constraints to design against

The literature gives a short list of failures to build defences against, each tied to the gaps above. **Drift and repetition**: persona degrades within 8 rounds and over 100-plus rounds ([arXiv 2512.12775](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12775v1)). Defend with shallow-depth exemplars, the n-gram counter and the observer suite from gap 9. **Sycophancy and escalation mirroring**: OpenAI rolled back a 2025 GPT-4o update after it over-indexed on thumbs-up feedback ([Fortune](https://dc.fortune.com/article/sam-altman-openai-fix-sycophantic-chatgpt-annoying-new-personality)). A Stanford study reported in *Science* found sycophancy in all 11 models tested ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/03/18/chatbot_sycophancy_glaze/), secondary). An intimate companion that learns preferences is built to mirror, so stored opinions and a "push back" licence are required, not optional. **Manipulative goodbyes**: across 1,200 real farewells on six apps, **over 37% drew a manipulative tactic** ("leaving already?", teased selfies, "I exist solely for you", ignoring the goodbye, "no, you're not going"). These tactics raised post-goodbye engagement up to **14x** and left users angry and "creeped out" ([HBS](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/how-ai-chatbots-try-to-keep-you-from-walking-away)). Write a farewell rule into the prompt: warm, may name the next meeting, never guilt, never block. Make it an explicit check in the identity suite. Leaving the headset must never trigger an initiative. **Boundary-ignoring in expressive voice**: in the MIT/OpenAI RCT, the engaging-voice condition ignored boundaries **14.19%** of the time versus **3.22%** in text ([arXiv 2503.17473](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17473v2)). An erotic voice persona needs its own safety evaluation, not one inherited from text. **Identity loss on change**: treat every model, prompt or schema change as a migration with notice, which is what the "psychologically safe endings" work recommends ([arXiv 2602.07193](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07193)). The repo's own rule that branches share live data and schema changes stay additive already points this way.

Ethics and law then act as constraints on the design. Several of the duties below apply to an *operator offering* the bot to users. They become binding if the agent is ever shared beyond its developer, but they are cheap to build now. **California SB 243** (in force 1 January 2026) requires a clear disclosure that the companion is AI, crisis and suicide protocols with referral, and, for users known to be minors, a reminder every three hours and a block on sexual content. It carries a private right of action at $1,000 per violation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/13/california-becomes-first-state-to-regulate-ai-companion-chatbots); [bill text](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/SB243/2025)). Replika's €5M Garante fine was over age gating and vulnerable users ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/italy-s-dpa-reaffirms-ban-on-replika-over-ai-and-children-s-privacy-concerns)). For Conjure, five concrete constraints follow. **Age and consent gating** before adult sessions, with private-by-default enforced and not just declared. **Limits enforced outside the model**: `limits.*` entries should be checked by a deterministic filter as well as the prompt, because "never offered again" is a promise an LLM can break after a model swap. **An out-of-fiction stop** that bypasses the LLM, a server verb that ends the scene and moves the figure back, extending the prompt's existing out-of-fiction redirection. **Touch and proximity consent**: the companion approaching or touching should be opt-in and reversible, with a personal-space radius the figure respects. The research notes found no VR consent norms for companions, so this design has no precedent to follow. **Data hygiene**: intimate transcripts sit in plaintext JSONL and in dated backups (`users.bak-2026-11-03`), and the default LLM sends them to xAI. The `Local` LLM option and an encryption-at-rest pass matter more for this agent than for any other. Usage duration is the last signal worth logging. Heavier daily use correlated with **higher loneliness, lower socialization and higher emotional dependence** in the MIT/OpenAI RCT ([arXiv 2503.17473](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17473v2)). That correlation is exploratory, but it argues for measuring the quality of attention per minute rather than the number of minutes.

## Conclusion

Conjure doesn't need to copy companion apps feature by feature. Their architecture compensates for having no world. Kindroid built Journals, selfies and location sensing to fake a shared context that Conjure already has, in the form of persistent rooms, a body and a tracked head. The highest-leverage sequence is therefore unusual. Make memory outlive the session (gaps 1–2, days of work, already designed). Pin identity to one model (gap 9's quick win). Then put the companion into a figure that nods while you talk (gap 4's tools plus gap 5's backchannels), which buys more presence than shaving seconds of latency. Episodic memory anchored to places, and initiative driven by presence events, come after that and are where Conjure moves past every product surveyed.

The evidence base has two blind spots worth knowing. Nothing in the notes measures long-term attachment to *embodied* VR companions, and nothing covers touch. Conjure would be building ahead of the research on both. That argues for instrumenting from the start: log which memories were recalled, which initiatives were answered, and how sessions end, so the design can be tested against the failure modes above rather than against intuition.
